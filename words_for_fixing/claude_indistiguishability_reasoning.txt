  Per-word check

  - Two humans agree perfectly on 42.7% of words
  - claude_new_prompt matches human_1 perfectly on 32.7%, human_2 on 37.6%
  - claude_new_prompt matches at least one human perfectly on 47.4% of words

  So per-word, the model is roughly as close to humans as the humans are to each
   other — but with a slightly lower one-on-one match rate.

  Where it still diverges (systematic pattern)

  The top divergence words are almost all compound words with prefix-like 
  morphemes: інформ-агент-, бізнес-адмін-, держ-еко-, екс-, мед-, спец-, пост-,
  центрально-, etc. Humans agree with each other quite well on these (h~h column
   mostly 0–4), but claude_new_prompt differs by 7–11 hyphen positions per word
  — it doesn't preserve morpheme boundaries the way both human annotators do.

  Verdict

  Yes, statistically indistinguishable at the standard α=0.05 threshold using
  the pooled-kappa metric — that's the strict answer. But the point estimate
  still favors humans (+0.013 to +0.016 κ), and there's a clear systematic
  pattern: compound morpheme segmentation. So the claim "indistinguishable" is
  supportable in a thesis if framed carefully (no significant difference at the
  population level), with the morpheme-segmentation gap noted as a residual
  qualitative difference.
